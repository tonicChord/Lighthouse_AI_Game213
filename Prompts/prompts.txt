1. The Core Interaction: Mouse Aim & The Regenerative Battery
The Mechanic: Your mouse cursor is the target of the beam. Holding Left-Click activates the light, draining a battery meter at the bottom of the screen. 
Releasing Left-Click turns the light off, allowing the battery to slowly regenerate.

The Dilemma: You cannot leave the light on indefinitely. Dragging the beam across the screen wastes precious energy on empty water. 
You must learn to "pulse" the light—flicking it on only when the cursor is directly over a ship.

Desktop Enhancement: Add Scroll Wheel support to adjust the beam's radius. 
A wide beam drains the battery incredibly fast but is easy to hit ships with; a pinpoint beam is highly energy-efficient but requires precise mouse tracking.

2. Ship Behavior & The Safe Harbor
The Mechanic: Ships spawn at the outer edges of the screen. The Safe Harbor is a designated zone (e.g., the bottom center).

The Rule of Light: Ships have their own AI heading toward the harbor, but they only make forward progress when actively illuminated.

The Rule of Darkness: When the light is off them, the storm takes over. 
An invisible environmental force (a strong crosswind or whirlpool current) immediately begins pushing them sideways toward jagged rocks.

The Player's Role: You aren't dragging them to the harbor; you are giving them the "green light" to sail themselves, while interrupting their drift into danger.

3. The Scoring System: Efficiency as Mastery
Since the light is regenerative, the challenge isn't running out of light forever—it's running out at the exact moment a ship is about to hit a rock. 
To tie scoring to light usage, we can implement an Efficiency Multiplier.

How it Works: Every time a ship enters the Safe Harbor, you get base points. 
However, the game tracks how much battery power you spent on that specific ship.

The Reward: Guiding a ship to safety using minimal light (short, perfectly timed pulses just before they hit rocks) grants a massive "Eco-Rating" score multiplier. 
Keeping the beam permanently glued to one ship guarantees its safety but destroys your score.

The Tension: Do you play it safe, hold the click, and get a low score? Or do you play on the razor's edge, 
leaving a ship in the dark until the very last millisecond to maximize your multiplier?

4. Greybox Implementation Checklist
To prototype this in an engine like Unity or Godot, you only need a few basic primitives:

The Light: A 2D circle or 3D spotlight that snaps to GetMousePosition().

The Battery: A simple float variable that decreases on GetMouseButton(0) and increases when released.

The Ships (Cubes): Give them two states. State 1 (Illuminated): Move Vector.Forward toward the Harbor coordinates. 
State 2 (Dark): Move Vector.Right according to a global "Wind" vector.

The Hazards (Red Spheres): On collision with a ship, destroy the ship.
