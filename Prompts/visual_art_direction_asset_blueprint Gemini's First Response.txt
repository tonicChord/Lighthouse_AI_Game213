# Beacon of the Storm — Visual Art Direction & 2D Asset Blueprint

Because you are building a top-down nighttime game where the player only sees small pockets of the ocean at a time, 
**visual design is not just decoration—it is gameplay readability.** When a player flashes their lighthouse beam for $0.3$ seconds, 
their brain must instantly recognize whether they are looking at a friendly Cargo Ship, a Pirate Raider, a Kraken, or a jagged Rock.

This guide breaks down every visual decision step-by-step so you can choose the exact look and feel of your game before we create or import external artwork files.

---

## Step 1: Choose Your Core Art Style (Pick One)

As a non-designer, the easiest way to establish a cohesive look is to pick **one** of these three proven 2D indie game styles:

| Art Style Option | Visual Reference Games | Look & Feel | Pros for Your Game |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Option A: Painterly Dark Nautical (Recommended)** | *Dredge*, *Sunless Sea*, *Darkest Dungeon* | Gritty, atmospheric, 
hand-painted brushstrokes with warm golden lantern light cutting through cold, eldritch ocean ink. | Maximum atmosphere; 
makes the storm and Kraken feel genuinely menacing while the harbor feels warm and safe. |

| **Option B: Crisp Illustrated Vector / Sea-Chart** | *Mini Metro*, *Bad North*, *FTL* | Clean, sharp geometric silhouettes with bold outlines 
and antique brass nautical chart lines. | Highest possible gameplay clarity at any zoom level; easiest to generate programmatically as crisp `.svg` / `.png` 
sprites. |

| **Option C: Modern HD Pixel Art** | *Sea of Stars*, *Hyper Light Drifter*, *Carrion* | Hand-crafted pixel sprites ($64 \times 64$ or $128 \times 128$) 
paired with smooth modern dynamic lighting and bloom. | Strong indie nostalgia; very distinct visual identity if migrating to Unity 2D later. |

> **Designer's Advice:** If you want tension and immersion, choose **Option A (Painterly Dark Nautical)**. If you want razor-sharp arcade readability and 
clean scalable files, choose **Option B (Crisp Illustrated Vector)**.

## Step 2: The Master Color Palette (Functional Color Coding)

In a dark game, **never mix warm and cool colors randomly.** We use a strict **60-30-10 Color Rule** where every color has a specific gameplay meaning:

### 1. The Environment (60% of the Screen — Cold, Dark, Receding)
The ocean and storm must stay in deep, desaturated cool tones so that ships, hazards, and light beams pop effortlessly on top of them.
* **Abyssal Midnight (Deep Water Base):** `#030914`
* **Trench Navy (Mid-Water Swells):** `#07162C`
* **Storm Slate (Wave Crests & Rain):** `#1E293B`
* **Lightning Squall Flash (Sky Tint):** `#93C5FD` (at $20\%$–$30\%$ opacity)

### 2. Player Light, UI & Safe Sanctuary (30% — Warm Brass & Safe Teal)
* **Fresnel Beam Core (Active Spotlight):** `#FEF08A` (Warm Ivory-Yellow)
* **Weathered Brass (UI Frames & Reticle):** `#F59E0B` (Rich Amber Gold)
* **Harbor Sanctuary Glow (Safe Zone & Locked Course):** `#2DD4BF` (Bioluminescent Seafoam Teal)
* **Sea-Glass Panel Fill (Transparent HUD):** `#040C18` at $22\%$ opacity

### 3. Threats & Entities (10% — High-Contrast Signal Colors)
* **Friendly Cargo Ships:** Warm Cedar/Oak (`#92400E`), Cream Canvas Sails (`#FEF3C7`), and a **Teal/Gold Prow Lantern** (`#2DD4BF` / `#FDE047`).
* **Pirate Raiders (Cannon Targets):** Charred Pitch-Black Hulls (`#18181B`) with **Blood Crimson Sails** (`#DC2626`) and an Orange Deck Brazier (`#F97316`).
* **Kraken Monsters (Light-Repel Targets):** Deep Eldritch Violet (`#7E22CE`), Bioluminescent Magenta Suckers (`#D8B4FE`), and **Venom Green Eyes** (`#4ADE80`).
* **Static Hazards (Rocks & Whirlpools):** Cold Basalt Grey (`#475569`) surrounded by **Bright Crashing Seafoam White** (`#E0F2FE`) 
so the edge of the hazard is unmistakable.

## Step 3: Game Object Design Blueprints (2D Top-Down Sprites)

### The Golden Technical Rule for 2D Sprites
Every moving ship sprite should be drawn from a **direct top-down overhead view**, facing **East / Right ($0^\circ$)** on a transparent `.png` or `.svg` canvas. 
This allows both HTML5 Canvas and Unity to rotate the sprite smoothly across all $360^\circ$ to match your `lastLightDir` vector.

---

### 1. Friendly Cargo Ships (3 Distinct Silhouettes)
Even without reading text, a player should know a ship's speed class by its **hull width** and **number of masts**:

* **Class 1: Swift Cutter (Fast / Agile)**
  * **Canvas Size:** $128 \times 96\text{ px}$
  * **Silhouette:** Narrow, teardrop racing hull with a long front bowsprit pole.
  * **Deck & Sails:** Light teak wood deck, **1 single billowing cream sail**, and a bright teal stripe along the gunwale.
* **Class 2: Merchant Brig (Balanced Hauler)**
  * **Canvas Size:** $128 \times 96\text{ px}$
  * **Silhouette:** Medium-width rounded hull with a raised stern cabin.
  * **Deck & Sails:** Medium oak deck with visible wooden cargo crates lashed to the center hatch, and **2 cream-gold sails** (fore and main mast).
* **Class 3: Heavy Galleon (Slow / High-Value Treasure Ship)**
  * **Canvas Size:** $144 \times 108\text{ px}$
  * **Silhouette:** Wide, imposing fortress-like hull with an ornate gold-trimmed stern castle.
  * **Deck & Sails:** Mahogany deck, emerald-and-gold canopy accents, and **3 wide canvas sails**.

---

### 2. Enemy Designs: Pirate Raider & Kraken

* **Pirate Raider (The Hunter)**
  * **Canvas Size:** $128 \times 96\text{ px}$
  * **Visual Hooks:**
    1. **Spiked Iron Bow Ram:** Instead of a wooden bowsprit, the front of the ship has a jagged dark-iron ram.
    2. **Tattered Crimson Sails:** Deep red sails with ragged torn edges and a stark white skull/crossbones emblem on the main sail.
    3. **Charred Wood Hull:** Scorched dark-charcoal planking so it looks immediately hostile next to the warm brown cargo ships.
* **The Kraken (The Abyssal Lurker)**
  * **Canvas Size:** $160 \times 160\text{ px}$
  * **Visual Hooks:**
    1. **Modular or Animated Design:** Best built as **1 central cephalopod head/mantle sprite** (with two glowing green eyes and mottled purple spots) 
	surrounded by **8 curved tentacle sprites** (or a 4-frame idle animation sheet).
    2. **State Change:** When hit by the lighthouse beam, its bioluminescent spots shift from **Violet (`#A855F7`)** to **Warning Coral-Red (`#FB7185`)** 
	to show it is being burned/repelled by the light.

---

### 3. Static Hazards: Jagged Rocks & Whirlpools

* **Jagged Basalt Rocks (3 Modular Variants: Small, Medium, Cluster)**
  * **Canvas Size:** $128 \times 128\text{ px}$
  * **Visual Structure (3 Layers in One Sprite):**
    1. *Bottom Layer:* Semi-transparent turquoise-teal submerged reef shelf (`#0D9488` at $30\%$ opacity) showing shallow water.
    2. *Middle Layer:* White frothy seafoam ring (`#E0F2FE`) that expands and contracts slightly.
    3. *Top Layer:* Sharp, angular dark-slate rock facets with crisp upper-left highlights so they look sharp and dangerous.
* **Abyssal Whirlpool (Rotating Vortex Sprite)**
  * **Canvas Size:** $256 \times 256\text{ px}$
  * **Visual Structure:**
    1. Pitch-black center "eye" (`#010308`) that fades outward into deep cyan-teal.
    2. Four logarithmic spiral arms of white/cyan froth.
    3. *Engine Trick:* You only need **1 static PNG** for the whirlpool! Rotating that single image continuously in code creates a hypnotic spinning maelstrom.

---

### 4. The Lighthouse & Safe Harbor

* **The Lighthouse Promontory ($256 \times 256\text{ px}$):**
  * A rugged granite cliff island with crashing white surf on its edges, a circular stone keep base, a classic red-and-white spiral masonry tower viewed from above, 
  and a rotating brass Fresnel lens dome that points toward your mouse cursor.
* **The Safe Harbor Ring ($256 \times 256\text{ px}$):**
  * Curved wooden breakwater piers/jetties forming a protective cove, lit by warm floating brass-and-teal lantern buoys around the $45\text{u}$ perimeter.

## Step 4: Typography System

To give the game a true nautical identity without sacrificing number readability during frantic storms, use this 3-font pairing 
(all free on Google Fonts and downloadable as `.ttf` for Unity):

1. **Display & Headers (`Cinzel` or `IM Fell English SC`):**
   * Used for *Harbor Ledger*, *Fresnel Beacon*, and floating callouts (`DOCKED!`, `CORSAIR SUNK!`). Gives an engraved brass-plaque feel.
2. **UI Labels (`Plus Jakarta Sans` or `Inter`):**
   * Used for button labels, instructions, and tooltips. Ultra-clean at small sizes (`10px`–`12px`).
3. **Live Numbers & Telemetry (`JetBrains Mono`):**
   * Used for Score, Shift Timer (`180.0s`), Save Ratio (`65%`), Wind Speed (`24 u/s`), and Spreadsheet values. Because every digit has the exact same width, 
   numbers never jitter when counting down.

## Step 5: Textures & Atmospheric Overlays

To make the flat 2D screen feel like a living ocean, you only need **3 lightweight texture files**:
1. **`ocean_tile_512.png` ($512 \times 512\text{ px}$ Seamless Tile):** Deep navy water with subtle hand-painted wave troughs that scrolls in the 
direction of the storm crosswind.
2. **`foam_caustics_512.png` ($512 \times 512\text{ px}$ Seamless Overlay):** Subtle translucent water ripples that scroll at a slightly different 
angle to create depth.
3. **`vignette_grain.png`:** A subtle dark edge vignette that frames the screen like looking through a storm-battered spyglass.
